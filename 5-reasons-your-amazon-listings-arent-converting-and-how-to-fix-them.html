5 Reasons Your Amazon Listings Aren't Converting (And How to Fix Them)

By Scott Nguyen · Published Jun 2, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. 1. Review the main image and supporting images
  2. 2. Make the title specific
  3. 3. Answer purchase questions
  4. 4. Review the complete offer
  5. 5. Use reviews as evidence
  6. A useful first review

A listing review should separate traffic quality from the information shoppers see. Compare the product with its own history and relevant category context before treating one conversion-rate benchmark as a target.

1. Review the main image and supporting images

Check the current image requirements for your category. Make the product easy to recognise and show what is included. Use the supporting image positions to explain scale, compatibility, features and appropriate use. Do not assume that an image style used by a competitor is permitted.

2. Make the title specific

Identify the product, brand and distinguishing attributes clearly. Follow the applicable title requirements and avoid repetition. The aim is to help the right shopper recognise the right product.

3. Answer purchase questions

Use bullets and descriptions to explain the practical benefit of a feature without inventing a performance claim. Size, compatibility, care, contents and limitations often matter more than adjectives.

4. Review the complete offer

Check price, availability, delivery promise, variations and eligible enhanced content alongside the copy. A content change cannot resolve an out-of-stock offer or a product that does not match the search.

5. Use reviews as evidence

Group recurring questions and complaints into themes. Improve the product or information where the evidence supports a change. Use only review tools and communication methods permitted by Amazon.

A useful first review

Record the ASIN, issue, proposed change and measure to watch. Review the result over a comparable period and account for changes in price, stock and advertising.
