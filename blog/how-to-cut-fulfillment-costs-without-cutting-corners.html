How to Cut Fulfillment Costs Without Cutting Corners

By Scott Nguyen · Published Dec 11, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Fulfillment costs are one of the largest variable expenses in e-commerce, and most businesses are paying more than they should. Here's where the savings are — without compromising the customer experience.

Right-Size Your Packaging

Carriers charge by dimensional weight, not just actual weight. A product shipped in a box twice its size pays twice the dimensional weight cost. Audit your packaging by SKU and eliminate oversized boxes. The savings compound across thousands of shipments.

Negotiate Your Carrier Rates

Request carrier or 3PL quotes using your actual parcel volumes, dimensions, destinations and service requirements. Compare base rates, surcharges, pickup and claims terms rather than assuming a standard discount.

Reduce Dunnage and Void Fill

Void fill material is a cost most people don't think about but it adds up. The solution is better-fitted packaging, not more void fill. Packaging that fits the product eliminates the need for excessive fill material.

Zone Skipping for High-Volume SKUs

If you ship a high volume of units to the same region, zone skipping — consolidating shipments at a hub closer to the delivery zone — can reduce per-unit shipping cost significantly. Ask your carrier or 3PL if this is an option for your top SKUs.

Reduce Your Return Processing Cost

Returns create transport, inspection and processing costs. Compare prevention and processing improvements using the actual causes, costs and customer impact in your business.

The next step: Review fulfilment cost per order and as a share of revenue alongside order mix and margin. Investigate the largest drivers rather than applying one cost threshold to every business.

Break the cost into components

Separate receiving, storage, pick-and-pack, packaging, parcel, returns, special projects and account fees. A blended “cost per order” can hide the process that is actually driving the increase.

Remove avoidable touches

Look for relabelling, repacking, manual order edits, duplicated quality checks and exception workflows caused by bad upstream data. Eliminating work can be more sustainable than negotiating a lower unit rate.

Review packaging and carrier fit

Package dimensions affect material and parcel cost. Test right-sized packaging and service levels against damage, delivery performance and customer expectations.

Use volume and profile in 3PL negotiations

Providers price based on workload, storage and predictability. Give a realistic order profile and ask which operating changes would lower cost for both parties.

For independent warehouse and cost review, see 3PL & fulfilment consulting.

Build a cost-per-order bridge

Compare the current period with the prior period and quantify what changed: more storage, higher parcel rates, heavier products, more returns, extra projects or lower volume. This shows whether cost increased because of rates or because the order profile changed.

Protect service while removing cost

Track error rate, delivery performance, damage and return turnaround alongside savings. A cheaper process that creates more customer problems can simply move cost into another part of the business.

Prioritize savings by controllability

Separate contractual rate changes from process changes the business can control immediately. Packaging, order batching and data accuracy may deliver faster savings than a full 3PL renegotiation.

Verify savings on invoices

After a change, compare actual invoices and cost per order rather than assuming the quoted savings appeared. New surcharges or volume changes can offset the improvement.
