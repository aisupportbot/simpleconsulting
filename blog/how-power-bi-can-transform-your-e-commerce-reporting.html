How Power BI Can Transform Your E-commerce Reporting

By Scott Nguyen · May 15, 2024 · 1 min read

Most e-commerce businesses make decisions based on data that's incomplete, delayed, or presented in formats that make analysis difficult. Power BI — Microsoft's business intelligence platform — addresses all three problems. Here's how to use it effectively.

What Power BI Does

Power BI connects to your data sources — your ERP, your marketplace reports, your advertising platforms, your inventory system — and creates interactive dashboards that update automatically. Instead of pulling reports manually and building Excel models, your key metrics are always current and always accessible.

The Most Valuable Dashboards for E-commerce

Revenue by channel by day, week, month, and year-over-year. Margin by SKU and by channel. Inventory days on hand by location. ROAS by campaign and platform. Return rate by product and by reason. These six dashboards, built and maintained in Power BI, give you the visibility to make proactive decisions rather than reactive ones.

Connecting Your Data Sources

Power BI connects to most common data sources through native connectors: Excel and CSV files, SQL databases, Shopify, Amazon Seller Central (via third-party connectors), Google Analytics, and most ERP systems. Building a data model that pulls all of these into a unified reporting structure is the core implementation work.

Sharing and Collaboration

Power BI reports can be shared with your team through the Power BI service, embedded in SharePoint, or distributed as scheduled email snapshots. This means your entire team can access the same current data without everyone needing access to underlying systems.

The fix: Identify the three reports you currently build manually most frequently. These are your first Power BI dashboard candidates. Building automated versions of these three reports alone typically saves 3–5 hours per week of manual reporting time.
