How to Create a Winning Sales Proposal That Closes More Business

By Scott Nguyen · Jul 3, 2024 · 1 min read

Most business proposals are documents that describe what you do. Winning proposals are documents that describe what the client gets. The distinction seems small — the impact on close rate is enormous.

Lead With Their Problem, Not Your Solution

Open your proposal with a clear articulation of the client's problem as you understand it. Not your solution — their problem. "Based on our conversation, here's what we understand to be the core challenge you're facing..." This demonstrates that you listened, that you understand their situation, and that what follows is tailored to them — not copied from a template.

Quantify the Value of the Outcome

Most proposals describe deliverables. Winning proposals quantify outcomes. "Based on the results we've achieved for similar businesses, we expect to improve your Amazon conversion rate by 15–20%, which at your current traffic volume represents approximately $X in additional monthly revenue." Now you're not selling a service — you're selling a return on investment.

Present Three Options, Not One

A single-option proposal forces a yes/no decision. A three-option proposal forces a which decision. Present a core option, a comprehensive option, and a premium option. Most buyers choose the middle. The premium option makes the core option look reasonable by comparison. The basic option anchors the price expectations.

Make the Next Step Explicit and Easy

The last section of your proposal should specify exactly what happens next. "To move forward, simply reply to this email with your approval and we'll send you the agreement and onboarding questionnaire within 24 hours." Remove every possible friction from the decision to proceed.

The fix: Review your last three proposals. Did they lead with the client's problem? Did they quantify the value of the outcome? Did they offer options? If not, rebuild your proposal template with these elements before your next submission.
