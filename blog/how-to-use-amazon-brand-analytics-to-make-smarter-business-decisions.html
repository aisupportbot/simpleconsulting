How to Use Amazon Brand Analytics to Make Smarter Business Decisions

By Scott Nguyen · Published Jul 7, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Amazon Brand Analytics provides aggregated search and purchase information for eligible brands. Start with a business question, then use the dashboards available in your account and marketplace.

Find the point of friction

Search Catalog Performance and Search Query Performance can help assess how products participate in the shopping journey. Compare impressions, clicks and purchase measures without treating them as interchangeable.

Use search and basket information carefully

Top Search Terms and Market Basket Analysis can support query and assortment research. Check what each report actually measures; clicked ASINs are not automatically the highest-converting ASINs.

Turn a finding into a test

Record the report, period, affected products and proposed change. Decide whether the next step is product information, availability, advertising or a broader assortment review.

Verify the available dashboards

Names, access and coverage can change. Refer to Amazon’s Brand Analytics overview and the reports visible in your own account.

Start with a business question

Brand Analytics can provide useful customer and search information for eligible brands, but the report should answer a defined question: which products need listing work, which search themes deserve testing, or where customers are comparing alternatives.

Use trends rather than one report snapshot

Compare consistent time periods and note promotions, stockouts, price changes and seasonality. A one-week movement may reflect temporary conditions rather than a durable change in shopper behaviour.

Connect search insight to catalogue and advertising work

When a relevant shopper phrase appears repeatedly, check whether the product page answers that need and whether advertising is already testing it. Keep listing and campaign changes documented separately.

Protect against over-interpretation

Marketplace reports are not a complete view of customer demand. Use them alongside sales, advertising, inventory, returns and customer feedback before making a major assortment or investment decision.

For broader Amazon analysis and execution, see Amazon management.

Build a repeatable monthly review

Choose a fixed reporting window and review the same products and search themes each month. Record what changed in price, stock, advertising and promotions so movement in the report is interpreted in context. Keep a short decision log showing which findings led to a listing edit, campaign test, assortment question or no action.

Use the data to prioritize—not to chase every signal

Focus on products with enough traffic or commercial importance to justify analysis. A low-volume term moving sharply can look dramatic without changing the business. Rank opportunities by potential customer relevance, available stock, margin and the team’s ability to execute a change.

Create a hypothesis before changing the listing

If the data suggests customers use a particular term or compare a certain product, state what you expect a listing or advertising change to improve. This makes the later review more objective.

Combine Brand Analytics with inventory reality

Do not chase an attractive search opportunity if the product cannot stay in stock or does not have enough margin to support the required advertising and promotion.

Share findings with the right owners

Search or customer insights may require action from advertising, catalogue, product or inventory teams. Put the finding into the operating queue instead of leaving it inside a monthly report.
