How to Use Negative Keywords to Stop Wasting Your Google Ads Budget

Every week that passes without a well-maintained negative keyword list is a week you're paying Google to show your ads to people who will never buy from you. Here's how to fix it.

What Negative Keywords Do

Negative keywords prevent your ads from showing for searches that contain specific terms. If you sell premium furniture and someone searches "cheap furniture," a negative keyword for "cheap" stops your ad from showing — saving your budget for buyers willing to pay your price.

Start With the Obvious Negatives

Every business has a set of terms that will never produce a customer. For service businesses: "free," "DIY," "how to," "template," "course." For product businesses: "used," "rental," "wholesale" (if you're retail), competitor names you can't win against on price. Add these before your campaigns even go live.

Mine Your Search Term Reports Weekly

The search term report shows every actual query that triggered your ads. Review it weekly. Any query that spent money without converting gets added to your negative keyword list. Any query pattern you see repeatedly gets added as a negative at the campaign level.

Use Negative Keyword Lists Efficiently

Build shared negative keyword lists in Google Ads and apply them across multiple campaigns. This means you only need to maintain one master list rather than adding negatives campaign by campaign.

The fix: Pull your search term report from the last 30 days. Add every irrelevant query that spent more than $5 without converting to your negative keyword list today.

Use search-term evidence, not assumptions

Review the actual queries triggering ads and classify clearly irrelevant intent, research intent, jobs, support requests, incompatible products or other mismatch. Do not exclude a term simply because it has not converted in a tiny sample.

Choose the right negative scope

Apply campaign-level or account-level exclusions only when the term is inappropriate across that scope. Overly broad negatives can block valuable traffic from another service or product.

Review conflicts after adding negatives

Check whether new exclusions interfere with important keywords or themes. Keep a reason or category for major negative lists so future account managers understand why they exist.

Use negatives with broader account structure

Negative keywords are one control. They cannot repair a weak offer, bad landing page or poor conversion measurement.

For search-term and account cleanup, see Google Ads management.

Maintain themed negative lists

Group recurring irrelevant intent such as jobs, free resources, support or incompatible products. Themed lists are easier to review than one long collection of unrelated exclusions.

Audit negatives after product or service changes

A term that was irrelevant last year may become valuable after a new service launches. Revisit large negative lists periodically so old exclusions do not quietly suppress new demand.

Review search terms before and after exclusions

Compare whether irrelevant themes decline and whether qualified traffic remains stable. This helps catch negatives that were too broad.

Assign list ownership

Shared negative lists can affect many campaigns. Document who can edit them and require a reason for major additions so accidental blocking is easier to diagnose.
