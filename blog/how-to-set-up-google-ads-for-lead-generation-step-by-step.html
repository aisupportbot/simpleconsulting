How to Set Up Google Ads for Lead Generation: Step-by-Step

Setting up Google Ads for lead generation requires a different approach than shopping or awareness campaigns. Here's a step-by-step setup that produces qualified leads efficiently.

Step 1: Define Your Conversion Actions

Before building any campaign, define what counts as a lead. A form submission? A phone call? A booking? Set up conversion tracking for each action. For phone calls, use Google's call conversion tracking with a duration threshold — a call under 30 seconds is rarely a genuine lead.

Step 2: Build Service-Specific Landing Pages

Each campaign should direct traffic to a landing page specific to the service being advertised — not your homepage. Remove the navigation menu from landing pages to prevent visitors from wandering away.

Step 3: Start With Exact and Phrase Match Keywords

Build your initial keyword list around 10–20 highly specific, high-intent terms that indicate the searcher is actively looking to hire rather than just researching the topic.

Step 4: Review and Iterate Weekly

Check your search term report weekly. Add irrelevant terms to your negative keyword list. Pause keywords generating clicks without conversions. The discipline of weekly review separates profitable lead generation from budget burning.

The fix: If you're running lead generation on Google Ads without call conversion tracking, implement it today. Phone calls are often the highest-converting lead type for service businesses and missing them means flying blind on your most valuable conversions.

1. Define the qualified lead and conversion value

Write the criteria for a useful enquiry, how the sales team handles it and what a customer is economically worth. This determines which conversion actions and bidding goals make sense.

2. Build campaigns around distinct services or intents

Group queries that can share a clear ad and landing page. Avoid putting unrelated services into one generic campaign where budget and search-term decisions become hard to interpret.

3. Set location, schedule and exclusions carefully

Use the areas the business can actually serve, then review geographic performance over enough data before narrowing further. Add negatives for clearly irrelevant intent.

4. Create a landing path that qualifies

Explain the service, who it fits, proof, next step and any meaningful qualification information. Forms should collect enough to respond effectively without creating unnecessary friction.

5. Verify measurement before scaling

Test conversion actions and confirm the account is optimizing toward the outcome you actually value. Review lead quality with the sales process after launch.

For setup and ongoing management, see Google Ads management.

Build a pre-launch QA checklist

Verify URLs, conversion actions, location settings, budgets, negative keywords, phone numbers, form delivery and landing-page mobile usability before turning the campaign on. Many expensive mistakes are configuration problems, not strategy problems.

Set the first review milestones

Decide when search terms, lead quality and budget allocation will be reviewed. Early data is noisy, so separate obvious errors from decisions that need more volume before changing direction.

Create a lead-response SLA

Define who receives leads, how quickly they are reviewed and what information is recorded. Advertising performance depends partly on what happens after the form is submitted.

Review search-term quality before scaling

After enough data arrives, classify terms into strong fit, ambiguous and irrelevant. Use that evidence to refine keywords, negatives, copy and landing pages.
