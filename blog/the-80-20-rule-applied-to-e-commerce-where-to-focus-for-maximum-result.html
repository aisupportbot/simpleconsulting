The 80/20 Rule Applied to E-commerce: Where to Focus for Maximum Results

The 80/20 rule is a prioritisation heuristic, not a fixed distribution to assume in every business. Use your actual contribution and workload data to identify where attention may have the greatest value.

Your Product Pareto

Pull your sales and margin data by SKU. In almost every business, a small number of products drive a disproportionate share of revenue and margin. These are your hero SKUs. They deserve disproportionate investment in listing optimization, advertising, and inventory depth. The long tail of products that generate minimal contribution often creates more complexity than revenue.

Your Customer Pareto

Compare customer contribution, repeat purchasing and servicing cost. Use supported evidence and appropriate permissions when deciding which retention efforts to prioritise.

Your Channel Pareto

If you're selling across multiple channels — Amazon, Shopify, Walmart, wholesale — pull your profit by channel, not just revenue. The channel that generates the most revenue often isn't the most profitable once you account for fees, returns, and margin. Invest in the most profitable channel first.

Your Marketing Pareto

Compare marketing activity using relevant sales or qualified enquiries, cost and measurement limits. Concentrate effort where the evidence supports it without ignoring the role of assisted journeys or new tests.

The next step: Review profit and workload by product, customer and channel. Identify a small set of priorities from the actual distribution, then test the effect of changing effort or investment.

Find concentration in the actual data

Rank products, customers, channels, campaigns or issues by revenue, contribution and workload. The “80/20” ratio is a heuristic, not a rule that must appear exactly in your data.

Separate high value from high volume

A top-selling SKU may create weak margin or high return cost. Use several dimensions before deciding what deserves more investment.

Protect critical dependencies

If a small group of products creates most contribution, review their suppliers, inventory, account health and fulfilment risk more frequently. Concentration creates both opportunity and vulnerability.

Use the analysis to stop low-value work

Identify reports, SKUs, campaigns or manual tasks that consume time without changing outcomes. Reallocate capacity to the work with clearer customer or financial impact.

Run the analysis on contribution as well as revenue

Sort products and customers by gross contribution after major variable costs. A high-revenue item can fall out of the priority group once returns, fulfilment or advertising are included.

Review workload concentration

Identify which SKUs, customers or channels consume most support, exception or reconciliation time. Sometimes the biggest improvement opportunity is reducing complexity rather than increasing sales.

Set protection plans for the vital few

For the products or accounts that drive disproportionate value, review supplier redundancy, inventory buffers, account health and service levels more frequently. Concentration should change risk management, not only marketing spend.

Re-run the analysis regularly

Concentration changes with launches, seasonality and channel growth. A quarterly review can show when a former hero SKU is declining or a new customer segment is becoming strategically important.

Do not abandon the long tail blindly

Some low-volume products protect assortment breadth, support bundles or serve strategic customers. Use the analysis to prioritize attention, not to delete everything outside the top group.
