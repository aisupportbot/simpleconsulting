The 80/20 Rule Applied to E-commerce: Where to Focus for Maximum Results

By Scott Nguyen · Aug 28, 2024 · 1 min read

The Pareto Principle — the idea that 80% of results come from 20% of causes — applies with remarkable consistency to e-commerce. Most businesses have more levers than they can pull simultaneously. The skill is identifying which 20% of levers drive 80% of the outcome.

Your Product Pareto

Pull your sales and margin data by SKU. In almost every business, a small number of products drive a disproportionate share of revenue and margin. These are your hero SKUs. They deserve disproportionate investment in listing optimization, advertising, and inventory depth. The long tail of products that generate minimal contribution often creates more complexity than revenue.

Your Customer Pareto

Not all customers are equally valuable. Your top 20% of customers by lifetime value likely generate 80% of your profit. These customers deserve different treatment — early access to new products, loyalty incentives, and proactive outreach. Most businesses treat all customers the same and leave significant retention opportunity uncaptured.

Your Channel Pareto

If you're selling across multiple channels — Amazon, Shopify, Walmart, wholesale — pull your profit by channel, not just revenue. The channel that generates the most revenue often isn't the most profitable once you account for fees, returns, and margin. Invest in the most profitable channel first.

Your Marketing Pareto

Which marketing activities generate 80% of your new customers? Most businesses are running 6–8 marketing programs and only 1–2 of them actually drive results. Identify the 20% and concentrate your budget there.

The fix: Pull your sales and margin data by product, customer, channel, and marketing activity. Identify the 20% driving 80% of profit in each category. Double down on those and cut or deprioritize the rest.
