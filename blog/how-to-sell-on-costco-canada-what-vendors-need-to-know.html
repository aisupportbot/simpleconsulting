How to Sell on Costco Canada: What Vendors Need to Know

A Costco Canada opportunity can materially change the scale and operating requirements of a consumer-products business. That makes preparation important before you commit to a price, pack configuration or forecast. Treat the opportunity as a specific retail programme with its own buyer terms and operating requirements rather than assuming a generic wholesale model.

1. Start with the item, not the full catalogue

Retail buyers generally evaluate a defined assortment or item opportunity. Build the business case around the exact product, configuration and region under discussion. If a club pack, bundle or exclusive configuration is requested, model the packaging, production and inventory consequences separately from your standard item.

2. Know the complete cost floor

Calculate product cost, special packaging, labels, freight, duties if applicable, warehouse handling, allowances, promotional commitments, expected deductions and the working-capital effect of the payment terms. Use the actual proposed terms rather than a generic “Costco margin” assumption.

3. Stress-test supply capacity

Large purchase orders are only valuable if the business can produce and deliver them reliably. Review supplier lead times, minimum order quantities, production capacity, raw-material risk and the cash required to fund inventory before payment arrives. Model what happens if the order is larger, earlier or more geographically distributed than expected.

4. Prepare for retail compliance

Retail relationships can introduce routing, packaging, labelling, ASN, EDI, invoicing and appointment requirements. Build a requirement matrix from the current vendor documents and assign an owner to each process. Do not rely on requirements from another retailer or an old programme guide.

5. Build a deduction and reconciliation process before launch

Keep purchase orders, shipment records, bills of lading, ASNs, invoices, receiving information and payment records in a structure that can be reconciled. If a deduction appears later, you need to understand the underlying transaction before deciding whether it should be disputed.

6. Protect the rest of the business

A major retail order can consume inventory needed for Amazon, Walmart, DTC or other accounts. Include channel allocation and replenishment in the launch plan so a successful wholesale order does not create stockouts somewhere else.

7. Treat buyer approval and commercial outcomes as outside your control

No consultant can guarantee that Costco will select an item, approve a supplier or issue a purchase order. The useful work is making sure the business understands the economics, can execute the operational requirements and knows what decisions still need to be made.

If you need help coordinating the operating work behind a retail-vendor opportunity, see retail marketplace and vendor onboarding support.

8. Build the vendor-readiness pack

Before a commercial discussion becomes urgent, prepare a working pack with product specifications, packaged dimensions, case configuration, production lead time, capacity, landed cost, insurance documents, logistics contacts, EDI capability and the internal owner for the account. The goal is not to send every document proactively; it is to know whether the business can respond when information is requested.

Questions to model before accepting scale

  • How much cash is required to build the first order?
  • What happens if production or freight is delayed?
  • Can the business support the order without starving Amazon, Walmart or DTC inventory?
  • Which packaging or configuration changes are unique to this account?
  • How will deductions, returns and invoice differences be reconciled?
  • Who owns buyer communication and internal execution?

A large order can expose weak systems quickly. The operational plan should be ready before volume arrives.
