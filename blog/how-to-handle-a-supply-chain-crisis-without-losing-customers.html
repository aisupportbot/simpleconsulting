How to Handle a Supply Chain Crisis Without Losing Customers

By Scott Nguyen · Dec 22, 2025 · 1 min read

Every e-commerce business will face a supply chain crisis at some point. The businesses that emerge with customer relationships intact communicate proactively and deliver on revised commitments.

Communicate Before Customers Ask

The moment you know there's a problem affecting customer orders, communicate — even if you don't yet know the full impact. 'We're aware of an issue and working to resolve it — update by Friday' is better than silence. Silence signals incompetence or indifference.

Give Realistic Timelines

The temptation is to give customers the best-case timeline to minimize immediate complaints. Resist it. If you promise resolution by Friday and it slips to Wednesday, you've compounded the problem with a broken promise.

Offer Customers Meaningful Options

When delays are significant, give customers genuine choices: wait with an incentive, cancel for a full refund, or substitute with an alternative. Customers who feel in control are more forgiving than customers who feel things are being done to them.

The fix: Draft a supply chain crisis communication template this week — before you need it. Knowing what you'll say when a crisis hits lets you respond quickly and confidently rather than scrambling.
