How to Improve Your Business’s Visibility in Google Search

By Scott Nguyen · Published Oct 16, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Ranking on page one of Google for relevant searches is one of the highest-value marketing investments a business can make. It's not fast and it's not easy — but it's absolutely achievable for small and mid-size businesses with the right approach.

Start With Long-Tail Keywords

Don't try to rank for "e-commerce consultant." You'll be competing against major agencies with millions of dollars in SEO investment. Instead, target "e-commerce consultant Mississauga" or "Amazon marketplace consultant Ontario." These longer, more specific queries have less competition and are searched by people with more specific intent — meaning they convert better anyway.

Create Content That Answers Real Questions

Create a useful, accurate answer to a question that matters to your audience. Content depth should match the task; word count does not guarantee a ranking.

Build Backlinks From Relevant Sources

Backlinks — other websites linking to yours — remain one of Google's strongest ranking signals. Guest posts on industry publications, mentions in relevant directories, partnerships with complementary businesses, and PR coverage all generate backlinks. Focus on quality and relevance, not volume.

Optimize Your Existing Pages

Before creating new content, ensure your existing pages are fully optimized. Each page should have one primary keyword, a meta title and description that includes that keyword, proper heading structure (H1, H2, H3), and internal links to related pages on your site.

The fix: Identify three long-tail keywords your ideal client would search for. Create one comprehensive, genuinely helpful page targeting each keyword. Promote each page through your email list and social channels when it goes live.

Replace the page-one promise with a search-visibility strategy

No one can guarantee a page-one ranking for a competitive query. Start by identifying commercially relevant searches where the site has a credible page, evidence and authority.

Fix technical access and page intent

Make sure the page is indexable, canonical, internally linked and clearly focused on the query’s underlying need. A technically perfect page still struggles when its content does not match the search intent.

Strengthen usefulness and proof

Add first-hand experience, examples, data, decision frameworks and transparent authorship. Avoid expanding a page with generic text merely to hit a word count.

Build topical and external authority

Support important pages with related internal content and earn legitimate references from other sites. Authority builds over time and cannot be manufactured safely with bulk links.

Use Search Console to find the next opportunity

Prioritize pages already earning impressions around positions 5–30, then improve title relevance, internal links and content where the data suggests a clear gap.

Build an opportunity queue from existing impressions

Export queries where important pages already rank around positions 5–30 and group them by service. These are often better improvement targets than brand-new keywords where Google has no evidence of relevance yet.

Compare the result pages directly

Look at what formats currently rank: service pages, guides, directories, videos or tools. Your page needs to satisfy the same underlying intent while adding credible differentiation.

Set realistic review windows

Search changes take time to crawl, index and settle. Record the update date and avoid rewriting the same page every few days because rankings fluctuate.
