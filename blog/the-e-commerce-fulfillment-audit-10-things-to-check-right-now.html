The E-commerce Fulfillment Audit: 10 Things to Check Right Now

A fulfilment audit should trace an order from inbound receipt through inventory, pick/pack, carrier handoff, delivery, return and reconciliation. The goal is not to chase a universal benchmark. It is to find the points where your own process creates delays, errors, unnecessary cost or weak evidence.

1. Receiving accuracy

Compare purchase-order or transfer quantities with what the warehouse actually received. Track shortages, overages, damage and time-to-available inventory. Keep receiving evidence for marketplace or supplier follow-up.

2. Inventory accuracy

Compare physical or cycle-count results with system records. Investigate recurring adjustments by SKU, location and process instead of treating shrinkage as a single annual number.

3. Order release and cutoff logic

Document when orders enter the warehouse, which orders qualify for same-day handling and what happens when inventory or address data fails validation. Marketplace promises should match the process the warehouse can consistently execute.

4. Pick and pack accuracy

Track wrong-item, wrong-quantity, missing-component and packaging errors. Review whether slotting, barcode scanning, work instructions or training can remove the repeated causes.

5. Packaging and dimensional efficiency

Check whether packaging protects the product while avoiding unnecessary dimensional weight and material cost. Include special retailer or marketplace packaging requirements where relevant.

6. Carrier handoff and delivery exceptions

Measure when the carrier actually receives the parcel, not only when a label is created. Review late acceptance, damage, address issues and claims by carrier and service level.

7. Marketplace and retailer compliance

If the warehouse prepares FBA, WFS or retailer shipments, audit labels, carton counts, ASNs, appointment records and proof of delivery. Keep the evidence needed to investigate receiving discrepancies later.

8. Returns turnaround

Measure the time from return receipt to inspection, disposition and inventory update. Slow returns can hide stock, delay credits and create customer-service questions.

9. Full cost per order

Include receiving, storage, pick-and-pack, packaging, parcel, returns, account fees and special projects. A low base pick fee can be misleading when the operation relies heavily on surcharges and exception work.

10. Escalation and operating review

Every repeated issue needs an owner, evidence and review date. Hold a regular operating review with the warehouse or internal team using the handful of measures that connect to customer and financial outcomes.

If the audit points to warehouse selection, pricing, SLA or handoff problems, see 3PL and fulfilment consulting.

Turn the audit into an operating plan

After scoring the ten areas, choose the three issues with the greatest customer, financial or marketplace impact. For each one, record the current measure, root-cause hypothesis, action, owner, due date and evidence that will show whether the change worked. Avoid creating a twenty-item improvement list that no one owns.

What to request from a 3PL

  • Receiving and inventory-adjustment reports
  • Order volume and exception reporting
  • Carrier handoff and late-shipment data
  • Return receipt and disposition records
  • Current rate card and special-project charges
  • Open claims, shortages or unresolved inventory issues

A fulfilment audit becomes valuable when it changes the way orders and inventory are managed, not when it ends as a scorecard.

Re-run the audit after corrective work

Use the same definitions and reporting window when you revisit the operation. If pick accuracy, receiving time or return turnaround improves, confirm that the change also reduces customer complaints, marketplace exceptions or cost. A metric that looks better but simply shifts work into another part of the process is not a real improvement.
