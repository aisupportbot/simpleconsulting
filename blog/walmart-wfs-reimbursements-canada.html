Short answer: Walmart Canada's public WFS guides confirm reimbursement in two specific situations. The first is damage or shortages caused during Walmart's own transfers between warehouses. The second is loss or damage in transit on inbound shipments sent through the Walmart Preferred Carrier programme, where a valid claim covers shipping fees plus up to 50% of the goods' value, capped at $4,000 per FedEx parcel shipment. For other gaps, such as units missing from available inventory, damaged receipts or returns that never come back, the public guides don't publish a reimbursement schedule. Treat those as cases to investigate and document through Seller Center, not as automatic credits.

This guide covers what happens to inventory once it is in Walmart's network. If a shipment was delivered but not fully received, start with WFS delivered but not received.

2. Set your return preferences before you need them

Return preferences decide what happens to returned and damaged units, so they shape what you can investigate later. Set them in Seller Center under Settings → Shipping Profile → Returns → Walmart Fulfilled Returns.

  • Defaults: restock sellable returns (free) and recycle unsellable ones.
  • Options: Restock, Return to me or Recycle/Dispose, as a default or by category.
  • Return to me requires a contact, a Canadian or US return address (a US address also needs broker details), a return authorization number of up to 12 characters, and both a parcel carrier (Canada Post with a policy number, FedEx or UPS) and an LTL carrier (Day & Ross). If carrier details are missing, returns default to recycling.
  • Damaged inbound units: the same settings page has a preference for inventory that arrives damaged at the fulfilment centre.

If unsellable returns are recycled by default, you can't inspect them to see whether the damage happened with the customer or inside Walmart's network. For higher-value products, having them returned is often worth the freight.

3. Reconcile monthly with four reports

Most gaps are only visible when you compare reports for the same SKU and period. Four sources do most of the work:

  • Inventory Health report: available units, damaged receipts, inbound units, units in inventory review, and units received in the last 30 days compared with units on your inbound orders. Units in review for compliance, expiry checks or cycle counts usually clear in 24 to 48 hours.
  • Customer Returns Report: return reason, current status, the fault column (for example, seller) and the date Walmart scanned the return as received.
  • Settlement report: the credits, fees and adjustments actually paid or charged.
  • Shipments page: inbound exceptions such as overages, shortages, damage, unexpected items, wrong location and incorrect prep.

For each SKU, roll the month forward: opening units, plus units received, minus units sold, plus returns restocked, minus removals, disposals and damaged receipts. That gives the closing units you should have. Compare it with available units plus units in review.

Illustrative example, not a client result: you start the month with 200 units, Walmart receives 150, you sell 120, 6 returns are restocked and 3 units are recorded as damaged receipts. You should have 233 units. Inventory Health shows 228 available and 2 in review, so 3 units are unexplained. Check the inventory logs and any later adjustments before treating those 3 units as a case.

4. Check customer returns closely

Returns are where small amounts leak away. For each return that hasn't been restocked or credited as you'd expect, note the RMA number, return order line, current status and received scan date.

  • Look at the fault column. If a return is marked as seller fault but the reason and your records suggest otherwise, raise it with Seller Support and include the RMA number.
  • Watch for returns that never arrive. A refunded order with no received scan date after a reasonable period is worth following up.
  • Track patterns. The same return reason on one SKU, month after month, usually points to a listing, packaging or product issue. Fixing that saves more than any claim.

5. Open a case that gets resolved

  • For inbound exceptions, use "Contact partner support" next to the exception on the Shipments page.
  • Raise one issue per case, with the shipment or PO number, SKU and UPC, quantities, dates, report extracts, photos, bill of lading and proof of delivery as they apply.
  • Respond within 7 business days. Walmart's guides say cases with no seller response can be marked resolved, and problem inventory can be returned at your expense.
  • Keep a log of the case number, date opened, units and value in question, latest response, next follow-up date and result.
  • When Walmart approves a credit, confirm it on the settlement report. Keep "case opened" separate from "credit confirmed".

The interactive reimbursement checklist and the reimbursement records checklist help you gather the right records before opening a case.

6. Prevent the next gap

  • Label and pack to Walmart's rules so units aren't refused or recorded as damaged on arrival. The WFS inbound shipment guide covers the requirements.
  • Photograph labelled cartons and pallets before pickup, and keep the bill of lading, packing list and proof of delivery with the shipment ID.
  • Consider Walmart Preferred Carrier for eligible inbound parcels, since it comes with a defined claim for transit loss. Its fee is discounted 100% until January 31, 2027.
  • Review return preferences each quarter, especially for higher-value SKUs.
  • Run the monthly reconciliation above, so discrepancies are caught while the records are fresh.

If you don't have time to reconcile WFS records each month, reimbursement and deduction review covers the records, cases and follow-up. You can also book a free call to talk through what you're seeing.

Canadian programme references

The rules above come from Walmart Canada's Marketplace Learn guides: handling common inbound exceptions, the Preferred Carrier programme, WFS return rules, rejected or returned inventory, the Customer Returns Report and the Inventory Health report. Your seller agreement and Seller Center may contain additional terms, so check them for your account.

Frequently asked questions

Does Walmart reimburse lost WFS inventory in Canada?

Walmart Canada's guides confirm reimbursement for damage or shortages caused during Walmart's inter-warehouse transfers and for valid claims on Walmart Preferred Carrier shipments lost or damaged in transit. For other discrepancies, the public guides don't publish a reimbursement schedule, so open a Seller Center case with evidence.

How much does Walmart reimburse for Preferred Carrier shipments lost in transit?

For a valid claim, Walmart's Preferred Carrier terms reimburse the applicable shipping fees and up to 50% of the value of the goods lost or damaged, to a maximum of $4,000 per FedEx parcel shipment.

What happens to WFS customer returns in Canada?

By default, Walmart restocks sellable returns at no charge and recycles unsellable ones. You can change this in your return preferences to have returns sent back to you, set rules by category, or dispose of them.

How long do I have to respond to a WFS case?

Walmart Canada's guides say that if a seller doesn't respond within 7 business days, the case can be marked resolved, and problem inventory can be returned according to your return preferences at your expense.

Which report shows damaged WFS inventory?

The Inventory Health report has a Damaged Receipts column for units that couldn't be received because they arrived damaged. Inbound exceptions, including damage, also appear on the Shipments page in Seller Center.
