How to Price Your Products on Amazon Without a Race to the Bottom

By Scott Nguyen · Published Apr 16, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Pricing on Amazon feels like a trap. Most sellers end up in a race to the bottom that benefits no one. There's a better way.

Price Is Not the Only Buy Box Factor

Review the complete offer: total price, availability, delivery promise and account performance. Use the current Featured Offer guidance for the marketplace, rather than assuming a fixed seller-rating threshold determines selection.

Know Your Floor

Calculate your landed cost, FBA fees, referral fee, and advertising cost of sale. Your floor is that number plus minimum acceptable margin. Never price below your floor.

Value Differentiation Earns Pricing Power

Better images, stronger A+ content, more reviews, and a clearer value proposition allow you to hold a higher price point. Build trust and you earn pricing power.

The fix: Calculate your true unit cost. Set a hard floor in your repricer. Any SKU priced below floor gets repriced immediately.

Start with a contribution floor

Build a SKU-level calculation using product cost, marketplace fees, fulfilment, inbound freight, storage assumptions, returns, discounts and advertising. That gives you a commercial floor before competitor prices influence the decision.

Separate price positioning from temporary promotions

A permanent price cut and a short promotion create different customer expectations. Record the reason, start date and end date for promotional pricing so the team can evaluate the outcome without accidentally turning a test into the new normal.

Watch the complete offer

Customers compare more than price. Availability, delivery, reviews, variation choice, images and perceived value all affect conversion. If a competitor is cheaper, first determine whether the offers are actually comparable.

Protect channel relationships

If the same product sells through retailers or distributors, consider contractual pricing rules and channel strategy before making marketplace changes. Use legal advice where agreements or competition-law questions are involved.

For help connecting price, listing, advertising and inventory decisions, see Amazon account management.

Create pricing guardrails before the market gets volatile

Document a minimum contribution threshold, preferred price range and conditions that justify a promotion. If automated repricing is used, set limits that reflect your economics and channel commitments rather than allowing software to follow competitors below your floor.

Review price changes with volume and margin together

A lower price may increase conversion but still reduce total contribution. Compare units sold, revenue, ad efficiency, return rate and contribution across the test period. Keep enough time in the test to avoid reacting to normal daily marketplace noise.

Set a price-review cadence

Review priority ASINs on a schedule tied to competition, promotions, inventory and cost changes. Avoid reacting manually to every small market move without checking whether the change is temporary.

Document cost changes promptly

When supplier, freight or fulfilment costs move, update the contribution model before continuing old pricing rules. A profitable price can become unprofitable even when the customer-facing price has not changed.

Include return and advertising sensitivity

Model how margin changes if return rate or ad cost rises. A price that looks safe under average assumptions may become fragile during a promotion or competitive period.
