How to Create a Business Continuity Plan for Your E-commerce Business

Business continuity planning — preparing for disruptions that could impair your ability to operate — is one of the most neglected areas in small business management. It's not exciting until you need it.

Identify Your Critical Dependencies

Map your business's critical dependencies: primary product suppliers, marketplace selling accounts, fulfillment infrastructure, payment processing, and key people. Each dependency is a potential continuity risk.

Amazon Account Suspension Preparedness

Prepare for account disruption by documenting responsibilities, maintaining relevant records and reviewing dependencies. Any response to a restriction should address the actual notice and current requirements.

Key Person Risk

If one person leaving would create a crisis, that's a key person risk. The mitigation is documentation — SOPs capturing critical processes — and cross-training ensuring no single task has only one person who can perform it.

The fix: List your top five business continuity risks in order of impact. For each, identify your current preparation level and one action you can take this month to reduce the risk. A continuity plan doesn't need to be complex — it needs to be honest about vulnerabilities and specific about preparations.

List the processes that would stop revenue first

Map storefront, marketplaces, payments, inventory, fulfilment, supplier ordering, customer service and key system access. Rank them by how quickly an outage would affect customers or cash.

Identify single points of failure

Look for one supplier, one warehouse, one administrator, one integration or one credential holder that could stop the process. Decide which risks need backup capacity versus a documented recovery procedure.

Write practical recovery playbooks

For each critical failure, document who declares the incident, how customers or partners are informed, what manual workaround exists and what data is needed to restore normal operation.

Test the plan

Run tabletop scenarios such as a 3PL outage, marketplace suspension, supplier delay or lost system access. Update the plan when the exercise reveals missing contacts, permissions or data.

For operating-process design, see e-commerce operations consulting.

Protect access and credentials

Document critical administrator accounts, recovery methods and authorized backups without storing passwords insecurely. A continuity plan should account for the possibility that the usual account owner is unavailable.

Set recovery priorities

Define which systems and processes need restoration first and what an acceptable temporary workaround looks like. Inventory accuracy, order capture and customer communication may have different urgency depending on the incident.

Protect critical data exports

Know how to obtain current orders, inventory, customer-service history and financial records if a primary platform becomes unavailable. Test exports periodically rather than assuming access will exist during an incident.

Create communication templates

Prepare basic internal, customer and partner messages for delays, system outages or fulfilment disruptions. Templates reduce response time while leaving space for accurate incident-specific facts.

Define return-to-normal criteria

Decide what must be reconciled after the immediate outage: missed orders, duplicate transactions, inventory movements, refunds and customer promises. Recovery is not complete when the system simply turns back on.

Review the plan after every incident

Capture which assumptions failed, which contacts were missing and what workaround created new risk. Update the plan while the experience is still fresh.
