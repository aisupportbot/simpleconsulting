How to Increase Average Order Value Without Discounting

By Scott Nguyen · Published Nov 20, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Most businesses focus on acquiring more customers to grow revenue. But increasing what each customer spends is often faster, cheaper, and more profitable than acquisition. Here's how to do it without discounting.

Bundle Products Strategically

Bundling complementary products increases the perceived value of the bundle while increasing your average order value. The key is choosing products that are naturally used together — not random items thrown together to justify a higher price. A bundle should feel like a convenience, not a forced upsell.

Introduce a Free Shipping Threshold

Setting a free shipping threshold just above your current average order value is one of the simplest and most effective AOV tactics. If your average order is $45, a free shipping threshold of $60 encourages customers to add one more item rather than pay for shipping.

Add a Product Recommendation at Checkout

"Customers who bought this also bought..." works because it presents additional value at the highest-intent moment of the purchase journey. The recommendation should be directly relevant and significantly lower in price than the cart total.

Introduce a Premium Option

Consider whether a premium option would provide enough additional value to justify its price. Model demand, fulfilment and contribution margin before adding complexity to the assortment.

The next step: Establish average order value and contribution margin. Test a relevant cross-sell, bundle or premium offer and judge it against both measures.

Improve the basket through relevance

Recommend complementary products that solve the same customer need rather than arbitrary add-ons. Use order history and product compatibility to identify combinations that make sense.

Use bundles with transparent value

A bundle can simplify the purchase or reduce fulfilment cost without requiring a blanket discount. Model contribution after packaging, fulfilment and return implications.

Set free-shipping or benefit thresholds carefully

Use current order-value and shipping-cost data to model the threshold. A higher basket is not helpful if the incremental shipping or product cost eliminates contribution.

Watch return and satisfaction signals

AOV tactics that push unnecessary products can increase returns or reduce trust. Measure contribution per order and customer behaviour, not basket size alone.

Use basket analysis before designing offers

Review which products are already purchased together, which first products lead to repeat orders and where shipping cost improves with an additional item. Build cross-sell rules from real purchase patterns rather than merchandising intuition alone.

Test contribution per visitor

A tactic may raise AOV while lowering conversion. Compare total contribution per visitor or per session when possible, especially for thresholds and bundles. The goal is a better economic outcome, not simply a larger basket among the customers who still buy.

Audit cross-sell relevance

Review whether suggested products are actually compatible and in stock. Poor recommendations can create friction or returns even if the basket value initially rises.

Segment AOV by customer type

New customers, repeat buyers and wholesale-like orders may have different natural basket sizes. Compare within meaningful groups before setting one universal AOV target.

Protect customer choice

Cross-sells and bundles should make selection easier, not hide standalone options or force unnecessary products. Customer trust matters more than maximizing one order metric.
