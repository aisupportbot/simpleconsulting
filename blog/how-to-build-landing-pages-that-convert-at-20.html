How to Build a Landing Page That Supports Qualified Enquiries

By Scott Nguyen · Published Jul 17, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

A useful landing page helps the intended audience understand an offer and take the appropriate next step. Conversion rates vary with traffic, offer and how the action is defined.

One Page, One Goal

The most common landing page mistake is giving visitors too many choices. A landing page should have exactly one conversion goal — book a call, download a resource, request a quote — and every element of the page should support that single goal. Remove the navigation menu. Remove links to other pages. Remove anything that doesn't directly support the conversion.

Make the Headline Specific

Your headline is the first and sometimes only thing a visitor reads. It needs to immediately communicate the value of what you're offering and who it's for. "Free 30-Minute E-commerce Strategy Session for Ontario Business Owners" tells the visitor in one sentence what it is, what it's worth, how long it takes, and who it's for.

Social Proof at Every Level

The higher the perceived risk of the conversion action, the more social proof you need. A booking form needs testimonials. A high-ticket lead form needs case studies and client logos. A free download needs email subscriber counts or a preview of the content. Match the weight of your social proof to the risk of the action you're asking for.

Reduce Friction at the Form

Ask only for information needed at this stage. Additional fields may add friction, but the effect should be tested with your audience rather than assigned a fixed percentage.

The fix: Build a dedicated landing page for your primary conversion action — stripped of navigation and focused on one goal. Send your next marketing email to that page instead of your homepage. The conversion rate difference will be significant.

Drop the universal conversion-rate target

There is no meaningful “20%” target that applies to every landing page, traffic source or offer. Define the action and traffic quality first, then establish a baseline for your own business.

Match the page tightly to the source

Use the same service, product or promise the ad, email or search result introduced. Message mismatch creates friction even when the design is polished.

Give enough proof for the risk level

A low-cost purchase may need product reviews and delivery information; a consulting engagement may need experience, case examples, process and direct access to the operator.

Remove unnecessary decisions

Keep the primary action obvious and reduce navigation or form fields when they distract from the intended path. Preserve alternatives only where they help qualified visitors.

Test the whole funnel

Measure form acceptance, booking completion, lead quality and sales—not only landing-page button clicks. Optimize for the business result.

Create a source-specific QA checklist

For every ad or campaign, verify that the destination page matches the offer, geography, price and next action. Landing-page performance can collapse when campaigns are updated but destinations are not.

Review form and booking completion separately

A landing page can generate many clicks on the CTA while losing users inside the form or calendar. Track the full completion path and test those embedded tools on mobile.

Use qualitative evidence

Sales-call questions, abandoned form comments and support messages can explain why visitors hesitate. Combine that evidence with analytics before choosing the next test.
