How to Launch a New Product on Amazon: The Complete Playbook

An Amazon product launch should be treated as an operating project, not simply a listing publication date. The listing, inventory, fulfilment, advertising, account permissions and post-launch ownership all need to be ready at roughly the same time.

1. Define the launch objective and marketplace

Decide what the first phase is meant to prove: catalogue readiness, initial demand, a specific assortment, profitable recurring sales or another business objective. Keep Amazon.ca and Amazon.com economics and requirements separate even when the product is the same.

2. Build the catalogue before inventory arrives

Prepare identifiers, titles, bullets, images, attributes, dimensions, weights and variation structure. Resolve category or brand requirements early enough that a blocked listing does not strand inventory after it reaches the fulfilment network.

3. Model contribution and launch spend

Use selling price, product cost, marketplace fees, fulfilment, inbound freight, storage, expected returns and advertising assumptions. Define how much launch spend the economics can support before setting campaign budgets.

4. Plan inventory and replenishment

Choose the initial quantity using demand assumptions, supplier lead time and the amount of working capital you are willing to commit. Keep a replenishment trigger visible from the beginning so the product does not sell through faster than the supply chain can react.

5. Prepare fulfilment and receiving evidence

If using FBA, maintain shipment identifiers, quantities, carton information and delivery evidence. If merchant fulfilling, confirm warehouse cutoffs, tracking, returns and customer-service ownership before the listing receives traffic.

6. Launch advertising around relevant shopper intent

Amazon Sponsored Products are CPC ads for individual product listings. Start with products and targeting that fit the offer, set a defined budget and review search-term evidence rather than scaling spend simply because the campaign is new.

7. Use only permitted review and customer-contact methods

If programmes such as Vine are available and appropriate for the account and product, check the current eligibility and terms in Amazon’s documentation. Avoid any launch tactic that depends on manipulated reviews, artificial orders or prohibited customer contact.

8. Review the whole offer after launch

Look at traffic, conversion, advertising, price, stock, returns and customer questions together. Keep a change log so listing, price and campaign edits do not become impossible to separate later.

9. Assign ongoing ownership

Someone should own listing issues, stock, advertising, cases, account notices and reconciliation after the launch week. That operating rhythm is what turns a launch into a sustainable channel.

Use the Amazon Canada launch readiness checklist or see Seller Central consulting for hands-on launch support.

10. Create a launch dashboard with decision thresholds

Track the small set of measures that determine the next action: listing status, sellable stock, inbound inventory, price, sessions or traffic, conversion, ad spend, attributed sales, returns and open account issues. Define what would trigger a listing review, budget change or replenishment decision before the numbers arrive.

11. Separate launch learning from scale

The first objective is often to learn whether the offer, listing and demand assumptions are working. Do not treat a small number of early orders as proof that the product should immediately receive a large inventory commitment. Expand only when the operating evidence supports it.

12. Document the post-launch handoff

If a project team created the launch, decide who owns the account afterward. Catalogue maintenance, replenishment, advertising, returns, cases and account health need recurring ownership once the launch checklist is complete.
