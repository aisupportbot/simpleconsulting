How to Use Email Marketing to Drive Repeat E-commerce Sales

By Scott Nguyen · Published Oct 2, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Most e-commerce businesses underinvest in email marketing while overspending on paid acquisition. Your email list is a direct line to customers who have already trusted you with their money — the highest-value audience you have.

Segment Your List From Day One

Not all subscribers are equal. A customer who has purchased three times is different from someone who subscribed but never bought. Segment your list by purchase history, product category, engagement level, and recency. Each segment should receive different messaging because their relationship with your brand is different.

The Automated Flows That Drive the Most Revenue

Welcome series, abandoned cart, post-purchase, and win-back sequences should be set up before you send a single promotional email. These automated flows run continuously and drive revenue without ongoing effort. Most businesses have at best a basic abandoned cart sequence. The others are money left on the table.

Promotional Emails Should Provide Value, Not Just Discounts

The business that sends a 20% off email every week trains its customers to wait for discounts. Instead, use promotional emails to introduce new products, share helpful content, feature customer stories, and educate buyers about how to get more from what they've already purchased.

Send More Frequently Than You Think You Should

Choose email frequency using consent, audience expectations, relevance and engagement. Test a sustainable cadence instead of assuming every list should receive weekly promotions.

The fix: Audit your automated flows. If you don't have a post-purchase sequence and a win-back sequence, build those before your next promotional email.

Start with customer lifecycle rather than campaign frequency

Map what a customer may need after purchase: onboarding, replenishment, complementary products, education or a reason to return. Build messages around those moments rather than sending the same promotion to everyone.

Segment using information you can maintain

Useful segments may include product purchased, recency, repeat behaviour or engagement. Avoid building dozens of segments that the team cannot keep accurate or interpret.

Measure incremental value carefully

Track clicks and attributed revenue, but also compare repeat purchase and contribution over time. A heavily discounted email can generate revenue while reducing margin or pulling forward an order that would have happened anyway.

Protect deliverability and consent

Use a clear permission basis, honour unsubscribes and follow applicable anti-spam and privacy requirements such as CASL where relevant. Keep list hygiene and sending practices part of the operating process.

Build a lifecycle map by product type

Different products create different post-purchase journeys. Durable goods may need setup, education and accessories, while consumables may need replenishment reminders. Create sequences that reflect the product rather than forcing every customer into the same automation.

Review suppression and fatigue

Track frequency by subscriber and campaign type. Exclude customers from messages that no longer make sense after a purchase or support issue. Strong email programmes protect the long-term relationship instead of maximizing sends.

Create a post-purchase exclusion map

Customers who just purchased should not immediately receive acquisition messages for the same product unless the message genuinely helps. Coordinate promotional and lifecycle campaigns to reduce contradictory emails.

Track revenue quality

Compare repeat contribution, refunds and unsubscribes alongside attributed email revenue. Aggressive discounting can inflate short-term revenue while weakening long-term economics.
