How to Set Up Google Shopping Campaigns That Actually Make Money

Google Shopping is one of the highest-intent ad formats for product-based businesses. But most Shopping campaigns are built wrong from day one.

The Most Common Mistake

Running a single catch-all campaign with all products lumped together gives Google total control over your budget — and Google will spend it on your cheapest, lowest-margin products.

Campaign Structure That Works

Segment into at least three tiers: hero products (best sellers, highest margin), supporting products, and long-tail. Set different bids and budgets for each tier.

Feed Quality Is Everything

Your product feed drives Shopping. Missing attributes, poor titles, or wrong categories will underperform regardless of campaign structure. Product titles should include brand, product type, and key attribute.

Negative Keywords Still Matter

Review available search terms regularly and exclude traffic that clearly does not fit the offer. Measure the impact on relevant conversions and margin instead of assuming a percentage improvement.

The fix: Pull your Shopping search term report. If you're seeing unrelated queries, you have a negative keyword problem bleeding budget daily.

Treat Merchant Center data as advertising input

Google uses product data such as titles, price, availability and other attributes to match Shopping ads to relevant searches. Product data quality is therefore part of campaign performance, not only a feed-compliance task.

Model contribution by product

Use selling price, product cost, shipping or fulfilment, expected returns and ad spend to understand which products can support paid acquisition. A campaign-level ROAS can hide unprofitable SKUs.

Segment the catalogue for decision-making

Group products in a way that reflects margin, category, brand, season or commercial priority. The structure should make it easier to change budget or bidding for a meaningful group rather than for arbitrary feed labels.

Review feed, campaign and landing page together

When a product has weak traffic or conversion, check Merchant Center diagnostics, price, availability, product data and page quality before assuming bids are the only problem.

For Shopping and product-feed campaign support, see Google Ads management.

Create a product-level profitability view

Join advertising spend with selling price, product cost, fulfilment and returns so the team can distinguish revenue-generating SKUs from contribution-generating SKUs.

Use feed diagnostics as an operating queue

Assign owners to disapprovals, missing attributes, stale prices and inventory mismatches. Product data issues should have a resolution workflow rather than being treated as one-off marketing problems.

Prioritize feed improvements by commercial impact

Fix disapproved or missing high-value products before spending hours polishing low-volume SKUs. Combine Merchant Center diagnostics with margin and inventory data.

Watch price competitiveness in context

A price gap can reduce conversion, but lowering price is not always the right answer. Compare margin, shipping, bundle size and brand positioning before changing the offer.

Coordinate advertising with stock

Reduce or redirect spend when priority items are at risk of stocking out. Advertising a product that cannot be replenished can create both lost momentum and poor customer experience.

Review search demand and product groups together

Identify which products and query themes create profitable demand. Use that evidence to refine feed titles, campaign structure and merchandising priorities.
