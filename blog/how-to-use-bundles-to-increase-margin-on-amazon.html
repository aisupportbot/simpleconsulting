How to Use Bundles to Increase Margin on Amazon

By Scott Nguyen · Published Aug 4, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

In this article

  2. Model the complete bundle
  3. Check the available programme
  4. Test a limited assortment
  5. Build the bundle around a real customer use
  6. Model contribution at the bundle level
  7. Check catalogue and inventory complexity
  8. Measure whether the bundle improves the business
  9. Validate packaging and fulfilment before launch
  10. Plan how bundle inventory is replenished
  11. Prevent catalogue confusion
  12. Review cannibalization

Bundles can offer convenience and a different assortment proposition. They should be evaluated on their own economics and the bundling rules for the marketplace and category.

Choose a useful combination

Use customer questions, purchase patterns and product compatibility to identify a sensible combination. Make the contents clear and confirm any packaging or labelling requirements.

Model the complete bundle

Include the component costs, packaging, fulfilment, fees, returns and advertising. A higher order value does not automatically create more contribution margin.

Check the available programme

Physical and virtual bundles have different requirements. Confirm whether a virtual-bundle option is available for your account, brands, country and products before planning around it.

Test a limited assortment

Set a defined test period and compare contribution, conversion and returns. Do not create duplicate or misleading product relationships simply to avoid a price comparison.

Build the bundle around a real customer use

A bundle should solve a complete purchase need rather than combine slow-moving inventory arbitrarily. Start with products that are naturally used together, confirm that the combined offer is easy to understand, and document exactly what is included so images, title, bullets and fulfilment records stay aligned.

Model contribution at the bundle level

Compare the selling price with the combined product cost, marketplace fees, fulfilment, packaging, inbound freight, expected returns and advertising. A higher selling price does not automatically mean a better margin if the package becomes larger, heavier or more expensive to fulfil.

Check catalogue and inventory complexity

Decide whether the bundle needs its own identifier, packaging or assembly step and who owns inventory accuracy. If the bundle is assembled by a 3PL or supplier, define how component shortages are handled. A bundle that depends on two SKUs can become unavailable when either component stocks out.

Measure whether the bundle improves the business

Track contribution, conversion, return reasons and inventory consumption against the standalone items. Keep promotional changes separate so the business can tell whether the bundle itself is creating value.

For catalogue and offer support, see Amazon listing optimization.

Validate packaging and fulfilment before launch

Confirm the bundle can be packed consistently, scanned correctly and shipped without creating avoidable dimensional-weight or damage costs. If components are assembled by a warehouse, document the assembly instructions and QA check.

Plan how bundle inventory is replenished

Track component availability and assembly capacity separately from finished-bundle stock. A strong-selling component can make the bundle unavailable even when other components are overstocked.

Prevent catalogue confusion

Images, title, bullets and package contents should all describe the same bundle. Clearly distinguish it from standalone items so customers understand what is included and returns do not rise because of expectation mismatch.

Review cannibalization

Compare whether the bundle creates incremental contribution or merely shifts customers from higher-margin standalone products. Use order mix and contribution, not unit sales alone.

Review bundle returns separately

Track whether customers return the entire bundle because of one component, and whether missing or damaged pieces create disproportionate service work. That feedback can change the bundle design.

Practical control: Add the bundle to inventory and replenishment reports as its own commercial offer while still tracking the underlying component constraints.
