How to Hire Your First E-commerce Employee: What to Look For

By Scott Nguyen · May 8, 2024 · 1 min read

The decision to hire your first e-commerce employee is a significant one. Get it right and you multiply your capacity and capability. Get it wrong and you spend 6–12 months managing performance problems that distract from growth. Here's how to approach the decision thoughtfully.

Define the Role Before You Post It

The most common hiring mistake is posting a job before you know exactly what you need. Start by listing the tasks that consume most of your time. Which of those tasks could be done by someone with 1–2 years of e-commerce experience? Which require your specific expertise? Hire for the former category — the tasks that are important but don't require you specifically to execute them.

Skills Matter, But Attitude Matters More

In a small e-commerce operation, your first hire will be asked to do things that aren't in their job description regularly. Look for candidates who are curious, adaptable, and comfortable with ambiguity — the specific platform skills they lack can be taught. The mindset can't be.

Test Before You Commit

Before making a full-time offer, give finalists a paid project — a real task similar to what they'd do in the role. This is more predictive of performance than any interview. How they approach the task, how they communicate during it, and the quality of their output tells you more than their resume or interview performance.

Set Clear 30/60/90 Day Expectations

New hires fail most often when expectations aren't clear from day one. Write a 30/60/90 day plan before your new hire starts. What should they be able to do independently after 30 days? After 60? After 90? This clarity benefits both parties.

The fix: Track every task you complete over the next two weeks. Categorize each as "requires me specifically" or "could be delegated." If more than 30% falls in the second category, it's time to hire.
