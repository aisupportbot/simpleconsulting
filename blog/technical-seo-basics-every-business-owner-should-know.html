Technical SEO Basics Every Business Owner Should Know

Technical SEO helps search engines access your pages and helps people use them. Start with the pages that explain your services and receive enquiries.

Check crawlability and preferred URLs

Confirm important pages load successfully and have crawlable links. Use a consistent canonical URL, keep the sitemap current and avoid unintentionally blocking pages or assets.

Review speed and layout

Use PageSpeed Insights to inspect performance and available field data. A lab score is a diagnostic, not a direct ranking score or a guarantee. Check loading, layout movement and interaction on real purchase or enquiry paths.

Check mobile and accessibility manually

Use narrow viewports, keyboard navigation and readable text. Test menus, forms and any embedded tools. Automated checks can identify some problems but do not replace using the page.

Use Search Console to verify indexing

Inspect important URLs and review indexing reports. A successful fetch or a sitemap entry does not establish that a page is indexed or ranking. See Google’s SEO guidance.

Make sure important pages can be crawled and indexed

Check robots directives, canonical tags, status codes and sitemap entries for the pages you want Google to discover. Use Search Console URL Inspection when a specific page is missing or showing an unexpected canonical.

Keep one clear canonical URL for each page

Redirect old duplicate paths where appropriate and use consistent internal links. Query parameters, print views or old article locations can create unnecessary duplicates when the site does not signal the preferred URL clearly.

Build internal links around site structure

Important service and topic pages should receive links from navigation, relevant articles and related service pages. An XML sitemap helps discovery, but it should not be the only way Google can find a page.

Watch broken links and status codes

Fix internal 404s, redirect chains and links to obsolete external resources. A crawler-friendly site also makes the visitor journey more reliable.

Use structured data accurately

Markup can help Google understand page entities and supported search features, but it should match content visible on the page. Avoid adding schema simply because a type exists.

Create a monthly technical check

Review Search Console indexing issues, sitemap status, broken internal links, unexpected redirects and important pages losing impressions. Small sites rarely need a complex enterprise crawl every week, but they do benefit from consistent maintenance.

Protect migrations and redesigns

Before changing URLs, navigation or templates, export the current high-performing pages and inbound links. Map redirects and verify canonicals after launch so a visual redesign does not accidentally destroy accumulated search signals.

Watch JavaScript-dependent content

Critical service information and links should remain accessible in rendered HTML. Interactive tools can enhance the page, but they should not be the only place important explanatory content exists.

Track important templates after changes

When the header, blog template or service-page generator changes, check a sample of URLs for titles, canonicals, schema, links and mobile rendering. Template errors can affect dozens of pages at once.

Keep technical fixes tied to business impact

Prioritize issues blocking important pages, creating duplicate content or breaking user journeys before low-impact crawler warnings. Not every audit item deserves equal engineering time.
