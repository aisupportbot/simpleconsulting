How to Sell on eBay Canada: Is It Still Worth It?

By Scott Nguyen · Published Feb 2, 2026 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

eBay may suit particular assortments such as collectibles, replacement parts or eligible used goods. Assess the specific product and seller economics before adding another channel.

Look for relevant demand

Review comparable items and available sold-item information. Distinguish asking prices from completed sales, and account for condition, shipping and completeness.

Use current Canadian fees

Check the category, seller plan, currency, final-value fee and any promoted-listing charges for your proposed setup. Do not assume a fixed fee range covers every item.

Define the operating process

Confirm inventory updates, handling times, delivery, returns and customer-service responsibilities. Prevent a one-off unit from being sold on two channels at once.

Pilot a manageable assortment

Compare the realised contribution and workload with your other channels. Expand only where the economics and operating evidence support it.

Evaluate eBay based on the product, not the platform’s age

eBay can still be relevant for selected categories, refurbished or hard-to-find products, collectibles and businesses that benefit from marketplace demand. The decision should start with your assortment and economics rather than whether eBay is newer or older than another channel.

Model the complete cost per order

Use current seller fees for your account and category, payment or promoted-listing costs where relevant, fulfilment, returns, packaging and customer-service effort. Compare the contribution with Amazon, Walmart or your DTC store using the same cost basis.

Check operating fit

Decide how inventory will stay accurate, how orders flow to the warehouse, which return policy applies and who manages buyer messages or disputes. A channel with modest sales can still be unattractive if it creates disproportionate manual work.

Start with a controlled assortment

Use a small group of products that are easy to fulfil and measure. Review sell-through, contribution, return rate and workload before expanding the catalogue.

For multi-channel operating support, see e-commerce operations consulting.

Check whether eBay adds incremental demand

Compare eBay orders with other channels by product mix, customer geography and margin. If the channel mostly shifts demand away from your own store or another marketplace while creating more manual work, the strategic value may be limited.

Define the maintenance workload

Estimate time spent on listing updates, customer messages, offers, returns and inventory exceptions. Include that operational cost when deciding whether the channel is worth keeping. A smaller marketplace can still make sense when the assortment is differentiated and the process is highly automated.

Create a category-level test

Choose a small set of products that fit eBay demand and give the test enough time to observe sell-through and returns. Avoid using clearance products only if you want the test to represent long-term channel potential.

Review listing maintenance requirements

Track how often price, quantity, item specifics or shipping information needs manual correction. A channel with healthy margin can still be unattractive if it requires constant catalogue intervention.

Use customer questions as channel insight

eBay buyer messages can reveal missing product details or category-specific expectations. Feed recurring themes back into the listing and broader product content.

Set an exit criterion

Define the minimum contribution, volume or strategic value needed to keep the channel. Close or narrow the assortment if the evidence does not support ongoing operating effort.
