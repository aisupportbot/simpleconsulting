How to Use Video on Your Business Website to Increase Conversions

Video can help explain a service or demonstrate a product. Whether it improves enquiries depends on the content and audience, so measure its effect rather than assuming it outperforms text.

The Most Impactful Videos for Service Businesses

A 60–90 second founder introduction on your About page is the single highest-impact video investment for most service businesses. Seeing and hearing a real person builds trust faster than any amount of copy. This doesn't need professional production — a well-lit, clearly audio setup at your desk is sufficient.

Client Testimonial Videos Beat Written Reviews

A client video can provide useful context when it is authentic and used with permission. Written testimonials can also be effective. Choose the format that helps the buyer evaluate the work.

Explainer Videos for Complex Services

If your service is complex or unfamiliar, a 2–3 minute explainer video on your homepage or services page reduces confusion and increases the quality of inbound inquiries. Visitors who understand what you do before they contact you are more qualified leads.

Technical Considerations

Choose a hosting approach that suits the asset and performance requirements. Lazy-load embeds where appropriate, provide captions that have been checked, and avoid making video a barrier to understanding the page.

The fix: Record a 90-second founder introduction video this week. Natural lighting, a clean background, a clear story about who you help and how. Upload to YouTube and embed on your About page. The trust impact will be immediate.

Use video where movement or explanation matters

Product demonstrations, service walkthroughs, founder introductions and complex before-and-after explanations can benefit from video. Do not add autoplay video simply because the page feels empty.

Keep the first seconds useful

State what the viewer will learn or see, then get to the demonstration quickly. Long branded intros increase abandonment.

Support accessibility and scanning

Provide captions where appropriate, useful surrounding text and controls that can be operated without a mouse. The main page should still communicate the essential information if the visitor does not play the video.

Protect performance

Use efficient embeds, thumbnails or lazy loading so the video does not slow every visitor. Test mobile performance after implementation.

Measure action after viewing

Track whether viewers continue to the relevant product, form or service rather than treating play count alone as success.

Script around one customer question

Each video should answer a focused question or demonstrate a specific task. If a script tries to explain the entire company, product range and founder story at once, the useful part usually arrives too late.

Create a text companion

Summarize the important facts on the page so users can scan, search engines can understand the topic and visitors who cannot or do not want to play video still receive the information.

Reuse production efficiently

A longer demonstration can often produce shorter clips for product pages, social posts and support content. Plan framing and permissions so the footage can be reused without re-shooting everything.

Create video-specific conversion paths

If the video explains a service, place the relevant CTA near it. If it demonstrates a product, link directly to the product or supporting details. Do not make the viewer search for the next step.

Review engagement at meaningful points

Look at whether users reach the demonstration or key explanation, not only total plays. A long video may need editing if most viewers leave before the useful content.
