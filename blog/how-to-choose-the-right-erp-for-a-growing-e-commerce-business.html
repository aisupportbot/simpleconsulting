How to Choose the Right ERP for a Growing E-commerce Business

Choosing an ERP is one of the highest-stakes technology decisions a growing e-commerce business makes. The implementation process is expensive, the switching cost is enormous, and the wrong choice can consume management bandwidth for years. Here's how to approach the decision systematically.

Define Your Requirements Before Talking to Vendors

Every ERP vendor will tell you their system can do everything you need. Before you start vendor conversations, document your specific requirements: What integrations do you need? What reports do you need to run? What processes are currently manual that need to be automated? What's your budget, including implementation — not just license fees? Walk into every vendor conversation with your requirements document.

Total Cost of Ownership, Not License Cost

Request a total-cost estimate covering software, implementation, migration, training, integrations and ongoing support. The relationship between those costs varies by project.

Evaluate Based on Your Industry's Requirements

Generic ERPs require significant customization to fit industry-specific needs. Look for systems with e-commerce-specific functionality: native integrations with your marketplaces and sales channels, inventory management built for multi-location and multi-channel, and order management that handles marketplace-specific requirements.

Reference Checks Are Non-Negotiable

Ask every vendor for three references from businesses similar to yours in size and industry. Call all three. Ask about implementation timeline vs promise, hidden costs, and whether they would choose the same system again.

The fix: Before contacting a single ERP vendor, spend two hours documenting your top 20 system requirements ranked by importance. This single document will save you months of wasted vendor conversations.

Map requirements by process

Document order management, inventory, purchasing, accounting, fulfilment, returns, marketplaces, retail EDI and reporting. Mark which processes are mandatory at go-live and which can wait.

Evaluate fit using real scenarios

Ask vendors to demonstrate your actual workflows: a partial receipt, multi-channel order, return, backorder, landed-cost update or retailer invoice. Generic demos can hide gaps that appear in daily operations.

Include integration and support cost

Compare implementation, licences, connectors, middleware, customization, training and ongoing administration. A lower subscription can become expensive when the system needs extensive manual work.

Assess change capacity

An ERP project requires clean data, process decisions and employee time. Choose a system and implementation scope the business can realistically support.

For requirements, vendor evaluation and system design, see e-commerce systems & reporting.

Score vendors against weighted requirements

Assign importance to financials, inventory, purchasing, order management, integrations, reporting, implementation support and total cost. Score every vendor using the same evidence from demos and references.

Plan for ownership after implementation

Decide who maintains users, master data, integrations, reports and process changes. An ERP without internal ownership becomes dependent on expensive external support for routine changes.

Interview reference customers with similar complexity

Ask about implementation overruns, support responsiveness, reporting flexibility, integrations and what they would do differently. A vendor-supplied demo cannot show the full ownership burden after go-live.

Define exit and data-access requirements

Understand how data can be exported, which customizations belong to you and what happens if the implementation partner changes. Long-term flexibility should be part of vendor selection.
