How to Sell Internationally From Canada: A Practical Guide

Canada's e-commerce market is constrained by population. The most significant growth opportunities lie in international expansion — primarily the US, UK, and Australia, where language, payment infrastructure, and regulatory frameworks are most similar.

Start With the US

The US market is 10x the size of Canada's, operates in USD (a currency advantage for Canadian businesses), and is accessible through existing channels like Amazon and Shopify with minimal additional setup.

Understand Regulatory Differences

Product, labelling and import requirements differ by destination and category. Identify the applicable regulator and requirements for the actual product before selling. A certification or approval in Canada does not by itself establish eligibility elsewhere.

Logistics for Cross-Border Fulfillment

Shipping from Canadian warehouses to US customers is expensive and slow relative to US domestic shipping. For significant US volume, establishing US-based fulfillment through Amazon FBA US or a US 3PL is essential for cost-competitiveness.

The fix: If you're a Canadian business selling primarily in Canada, calculate what your revenue would look like at your current conversion rate with US traffic. If the number is significant, US expansion warrants a structured evaluation.

Choose the destination based on demand and operating feasibility

Start with countries where the product has a credible customer opportunity and where the business can support shipping, returns, tax, product compliance and customer service. Do not assume that international demand automatically makes every market economical.

Build a landed-cost model

Include product cost, freight, duties or tariffs where applicable, brokerage, taxes, marketplace or payment fees, fulfilment, returns and currency effects. Test a slower-sales scenario because inventory can become expensive when it is placed far from the core business.

Review product and consumer requirements

Labelling, safety, language, warranty and other obligations vary by product and jurisdiction. Use qualified customs, tax and legal specialists for requirements outside your expertise.

Design returns and customer service before launch

Decide where returns go, who pays freight, how refunds are handled and what support hours or languages are required. A market can look attractive until reverse logistics are included.

For cross-border operating support, see e-commerce operations consulting.

Decide where inventory should physically sit

Compare cross-border fulfilment from Canada with placing inventory in the destination market. Consider transit time, duties, parcel cost, returns and working capital. A local inventory position can improve service but increases the amount of cash committed outside the home market.

Build a country launch checklist

Track tax setup, product requirements, translations where needed, payment methods, fulfilment, returns, customer service and reporting by country. Treat each market as a separate operating launch rather than a simple currency switch on the website.

Start with one market and a defined assortment

A controlled launch makes tax, shipping, returns and compliance easier to learn. Expand the assortment after the business understands actual landed cost and customer-service workload.

Create country-specific operating ownership

Assign responsibility for pricing, inventory, marketplace issues, returns and compliance by market. International growth becomes risky when every problem is treated as an exception with no owner.

Set a country exit rule

Define the minimum volume, contribution or strategic value needed to keep supporting a market. International complexity should be justified by evidence, not sunk cost.
