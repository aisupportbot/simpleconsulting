How to Reduce Customer Service Tickets With Better Product Information

By Scott Nguyen · Published Jun 16, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Repeated customer questions can reveal missing or unclear product information. Establish the actual pattern from your own tickets before setting a reduction target.

Audit Your Ticket Themes

Export a suitable sample of recent tickets and group them by question. Use the actual counts to identify which topics deserve attention first.

Fix the Listing First

If customers repeatedly ask about size, compatibility, or usage, that information needs to be prominent in your listing — not buried in the description.

Build a FAQ Section

Build your FAQ directly from ticket data — questions customers actually ask, not ones you assume they have.

Use Post-Purchase Emails Proactively

A post-purchase email answering the most common questions before customers need to ask dramatically reduces ticket volume.

The fix: Pull your top 10 ticket categories. For each, identify whether a listing update, FAQ, or post-purchase email would have prevented it. Implement fixes for your top two this month.

Start with the actual reasons customers contact you

Group support tickets by theme: compatibility, size, setup, delivery, missing components, warranty, returns or another repeat question. Count volume and product concentration before deciding what information to change.

Fix the highest-friction information gap

Move answers into the places customers use before contacting support: product pages, marketplace bullets, images, packaging inserts, setup guides or post-purchase messages where permitted. The best format depends on when the question occurs.

Keep product information consistent across channels

A customer should not see one compatibility statement on Amazon and another on Shopify. Maintain a source-of-truth product record and publish approved wording across marketplaces, retail partners and support documents.

Measure ticket quality after the change

Compare the same issue category before and after the content update. Total tickets may rise with sales, so consider tickets per order or per unit sold where useful.

For marketplace catalogue work, see Amazon listing optimization or Walmart listing optimization.

Create a content-owner matrix

For each recurring ticket theme, identify where the answer should live and who owns it: product page, marketplace listing, packaging, setup guide, FAQ or post-purchase email. This prevents the support team from repeatedly solving a problem the content team could remove upstream.

Review ticket deflection without hiding problems

A lower ticket count is only positive when customers still get the information they need. Watch returns, negative reviews and repeat contacts so the business does not “reduce support” by making help harder to reach.

Build a top-question dashboard

Track the most frequent questions by product, channel and stage of ownership. A question before purchase may need a listing fix, while a post-purchase setup question may belong in instructions or onboarding content.

Use self-service without removing human support

Make common answers easy to find while keeping an escalation path for issues that are complex, sensitive or safety-related. Deflection should improve convenience, not block customers from help.

Review the cost of recurring questions

Estimate contact volume and handling time for the largest themes. This helps prioritize content fixes that free meaningful support capacity.

Feed findings into product development

When information cannot solve the confusion, the product design or packaging may need to change. Treat support data as product evidence, not only a service metric.
