How to Write Ad Copy That Gets Clicked Without Overpromising

By Scott Nguyen · Aug 18, 2025 · 1 min read

The goal of ad copy isn't maximum clicks — it's maximum qualified clicks. Copy that overpromises gets clicks from everyone and converts poorly. Specific, honest copy gets clicks from buyers and converts at a high rate.

Lead With the Outcome, Not the Feature

'Certified Google Ads Management' describes your service. 'Stop Wasting Your Google Ads Budget — Results in 30 Days' describes the buyer's outcome. The outcome-led headline gets clicks from people who have the problem you solve.

Use Numbers and Specificity

'42% Average Revenue Increase' is more compelling than 'Significant Revenue Growth.' Specificity signals that your claims are based on real results, not marketing language.

Address the Objection in the Ad

What's the most common reason your target buyer hesitates? Address it directly. 'No Long-Term Contracts' removes the risk objection. 'Ontario-Based Team' removes the trust objection for local buyers.

The fix: Review your current ad headlines. Rewrite your worst-performing headline to lead with a specific outcome and a number. Measure the CTR change over 30 days.
