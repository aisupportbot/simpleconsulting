How to Write Ad Copy That Gets Clicked Without Overpromising

By Scott Nguyen · Published Aug 18, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Good ad copy helps the right customer understand your offer. Specificity is useful when it describes work you actually do and claims you can support.

Describe the service and next step

“Review your Google Ads campaigns with Scott” explains an action. “Results in 30 days” promises an outcome that may not be supportable. State the scope, duration or deliverable you can actually provide.

Use evidence for results claims

Publish a percentage or financial outcome only when the records support it and any required permission has been obtained. Include the time period and context. Do not invent an average or use a hypothetical number as a client result.

Address practical questions

Explain who performs the work, how the first conversation works and when the fee is agreed. Use contract or certification claims only when they accurately describe the current offer.

Measure enquiry quality

Compare qualified leads and cost alongside click-through rate. More clicks are not useful if the ad attracts people who need a different service.

Match the language to the search intent

Use the service, product or problem the user searched for in a natural way, then explain the specific value of the offer. Avoid vague superlatives that could apply to any advertiser.

Use claims you can support

Prices, guarantees, rankings, timeframes and performance claims should be current and verifiable. If the landing page does not substantiate the message, the ad creates both trust and policy risk.

Build variety around real differentiators

Responsive Search Ads can combine assets in multiple ways, so write headlines and descriptions that still make sense when mixed. Use differentiators such as direct access, product range, location or service model rather than repeating the same phrase.

Judge copy with downstream quality

A high clickthrough rate is not automatically a success if the clicks do not become qualified leads or profitable orders. Review query, ad and landing-page performance together.

Create an approved claims library

Maintain a list of product facts, service differentiators, pricing language and proof that marketing teams can use safely. This speeds up ad creation while reducing the risk of unsupported or outdated claims.

Review asset combinations in context

For responsive formats, read likely headline and description combinations as complete ads. A set of individually strong assets can still create awkward or repetitive messages when combined.

Create message themes before individual assets

Group approved ideas into themes such as service scope, expertise, convenience, price transparency or specific product benefits. This produces meaningful variation instead of dozens of headlines that say the same thing.

Match urgency to reality

Use deadlines, scarcity or “limited” language only when the condition is genuine and current. Manufactured urgency can damage trust and may create policy risk.

Review competitor sameness

Search the important queries and note repeated claims across advertisers. Use factual differentiators that the business can prove rather than copying the most common wording.

Track lead quality by message theme where possible

A headline can increase clicks but attract the wrong customer. Compare downstream outcomes before declaring an asset successful.
