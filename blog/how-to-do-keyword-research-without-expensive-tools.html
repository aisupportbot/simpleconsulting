How to Do Keyword Research Without Expensive Tools

By Scott Nguyen · Published Feb 26, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-28 · 3 min read

Keyword research can begin with customer questions, your existing search data and free research tools. Use these to build a list of relevant topics before paying for additional data.

Start With Google's Own Suggestions

Autocomplete and related searches can suggest how people phrase a topic. Treat them as research prompts, not verified search-volume data or a guarantee that every phrase is relevant to your buyers.

Google Search Console Is Free and Invaluable

If your site is already live, Google Search Console shows you exactly which queries are bringing visitors to your site — and which pages they land on. This is real data from your actual audience, not a tool's estimate. Filter by queries where you're ranking in positions 5–20. Those are your fastest opportunities to move to page one.

Study Your Competitors

Type your competitor's URL into Google's free keyword planner (requires a Google Ads account, but you don't need to run ads). It shows you which keywords Google associates with their site. Take the most relevant ones for your own content.

Answer the Public and Reddit

Answer the Public (free tier available) shows you questions people ask about any topic. Reddit searches show you what your target audience actually discusses, in their own language. Both are free and incredibly useful for content ideas.

The fix: Spend 30 minutes with Google Autocomplete and Search Console before buying any tool. You'll find more actionable keywords than most paid tools provide.

Start with the services and problems you sell

Write down the phrases a customer might use when they already know the problem: service names, platforms, errors, comparisons and “consultant” or “management” language. Commercial relevance matters more than a giant keyword list.

Use Google Search and Search Console

Autocomplete, related searches and the actual queries in Search Console can reveal language customers use. Search Console is especially valuable because it shows terms for which your site is already being tested.

Map one primary intent to one strong page

Group closely related phrases that can be answered by the same service or article. Avoid creating a separate page for every word variation when the search intent is identical.

Prioritize by business value and attainable evidence

A lower-volume query with strong buying intent may be more valuable than a broad informational term. Use current rankings and competitor pages to decide where a better page could reasonably compete.

Revisit the map with real performance data

After publishing, use impressions, clicks and queries to refine titles, content and internal links rather than relying forever on the original keyword list.

Create a keyword-to-page map

Assign each meaningful query cluster to an existing or planned page. This reveals cannibalization risks and prevents multiple articles from targeting the same intent accidentally.

Use competitor gaps carefully

A competitor ranking for a keyword does not mean you should target it. Check whether the search connects to a service you offer and whether you can provide credible expertise.

Record the evidence behind prioritization

For each opportunity, save impressions, current position, commercial relevance and the page you plan to improve. This makes SEO planning easier to revisit later.
