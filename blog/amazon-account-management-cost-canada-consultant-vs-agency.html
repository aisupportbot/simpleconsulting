Amazon Account Management Cost in Canada: Consultant vs Agency

When you search for Amazon account management services in Canada, you'll find independent consultants, specialist agencies and larger full-service firms. They do not all sell the same thing. A quote for a one-time account review cannot be compared fairly with a quote for managing listings, advertising, inventory and Seller Central cases every week.

There is no single dependable price for Amazon account management. The cost depends on the marketplaces you operate in, how many SKUs need attention, the condition of your account and how much responsibility you want someone else to take on. The better question is what you are paying someone to handle, how the fee is calculated and how you'll know the work is being done.

1. What does Amazon account management include?

Before comparing prices, separate your needs into a few practical areas:

  • Seller Central operations: account notices, catalogue updates, listing issues, variation structure, inventory and FBA shipment follow-up.
  • Advertising: campaign maintenance, targeting, search-term analysis, budgets, reporting and coordination with listing and stock decisions.
  • Vendor Central: purchase orders, shipping documents, shortages, deductions and account reporting. This is not the same operating model as Seller Central.
  • Planning and reporting: defined priorities, product economics, stock reviews, action logs and communication with your internal team.

Ask the provider to mark which tasks are included, excluded or billed separately. Account management sounds comprehensive, but the actual scope can vary considerably.

2. Common Amazon consultant and agency pricing models

Hourly consulting

Hourly billing can work well for a defined Seller Central problem, an account review or a backlog that has not been scoped yet. Confirm the hourly rate, expected hours, what requires your approval and whether there is a minimum engagement.

Fixed-fee project

For a clearly defined deliverable—such as diagnosing a listing problem or reviewing an existing advertising setup—a project fee can make budgeting easier. Ask what happens if new issues are uncovered and how revisions or extra account work are priced.

Monthly retainer

Ongoing account management is often structured as a monthly fee for a defined set of responsibilities and reporting. The number of products, countries, sales channels and stakeholders can change the workload substantially. Ask who will actually do the work and how priorities are handled when urgent problems arise.

Percentage-of-sales or advertising-spend fees

Some providers charge fees linked to revenue or advertising spend, sometimes with minimums. Understand which sales are included, whether attribution comes from ads or total marketplace revenue, and whether the arrangement rewards work that improves contribution—not simply more spending.

Don't compare quotes using only the monthly total. Compare deliverables, limits, access, reporting, change controls and the amount of coordination still left to your team.

3. What makes the cost go up or down?

In practice, workload matters more than a generic package label. Consider:

  • Catalogue size and complexity: a few established products require different maintenance from hundreds of variations or frequent launches.
  • Account condition: an established, organised catalogue is less intensive than one with unresolved suppression, data, fulfilment or policy issues.
  • Marketplaces and account models: Amazon.ca and Amazon.com can require separate pricing, compliance, inventory and campaign work; Vendor Central adds distinct wholesale processes.
  • Advertising scope: campaign oversight is not identical to full PPC management, creative production and landing-page testing.
  • Operating responsibility: advice given to your team is different from a consultant actually managing cases, updates, reporting and follow-through.

4. Agency vs independent Amazon consultant: which is right for you?

An independent Amazon consultant can be a good fit when you want a clear point of contact, direct involvement from the person you hire, and support tailored to a particular operating problem. Ask how work is covered when that person is unavailable and what falls outside their expertise.

An Amazon account management agency may be a better fit when you need a coordinated team covering creative, paid media, catalogue production and multiple marketplaces. Ask whether specialist roles are included or contracted out, and who is accountable when work moves between teams.

Neither model guarantees results. The right choice depends on your internal capacity, the urgency of the work and the gap you're trying to fill. A business that needs one hard Seller Central problem resolved may not need a full agency. A brand launching hundreds of products across countries may need more than one person.

5. Questions to ask before signing a contract

  • Who will actually work on the account and be my day-to-day contact?
  • Which activities are included each month, and what triggers additional charges?
  • Who approves changes to pricing, advertising budgets, listings or inventory?
  • How will you measure progress, and which reports will I receive?
  • How are marketplace cases, deductions, suppressed listings and stock issues handled?
  • What access is required, and can it be limited to appropriate account permissions?
  • What are the cancellation terms and how will the account be handed back?

Request a written scope with deliverables and boundaries. Be cautious about guarantees of rankings, sales or account reinstatement: those outcomes depend on factors a provider does not fully control.

6. When is ongoing management worth paying for?

Start by listing the account work that currently gets delayed and what that delay costs in staff time, lost availability, advertising inefficiency or unresolved issues. If the work is recurring and someone must own it each week, ongoing management may be worth comparing with hiring or reallocating an internal employee.

If the problem is isolated, start smaller. A diagnostic engagement can establish the actual cause and prevent you from committing to a broad retainer before you know what needs fixing.

7. A sensible way to request a quote

Prepare a brief description of your marketplace and account model, approximate number of active SKUs, main bottlenecks, who currently owns operations and whether you need a one-time review or recurring support. Share enough to scope the work, but do not send account passwords, customer records or sensitive documents through a public contact form.

Frequently asked questions

Is an Amazon account manager the same as an Amazon PPC manager?

No. PPC management focuses on advertising. Broader account management may also include listings, account health, stock, fulfilment, catalogue and reporting. Confirm the exact scope.

Is a monthly retainer always cheaper than hourly consulting?

No. A retainer can be convenient when the workload recurs, while hourly work may suit a limited task. Compare the expected work, minimum commitments, extra fees and reporting, not only the billing label.

Does Amazon account management include Seller Central and Vendor Central?

Not automatically. They are different account models and not every provider covers both. Ask which marketplaces and workflows are supported.

Can someone guarantee higher Amazon sales?

No provider controls customer demand, marketplace decisions, competition or every part of the offer. Ask for a clear improvement plan, agreed reporting and realistic responsibilities instead of promises.
