How to Do Keyword Research Without Expensive Tools

By Scott Nguyen · Published Feb 26, 2025 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. Start With Google's Own Suggestions
  2. Google Search Console Is Free and Invaluable
  3. Study Your Competitors
  4. Answer the Public and Reddit

Keyword research can begin with customer questions, your existing search data and free research tools. Use these to build a list of relevant topics before paying for additional data.

Start With Google's Own Suggestions

Autocomplete and related searches can suggest how people phrase a topic. Treat them as research prompts, not verified search-volume data or a guarantee that every phrase is relevant to your buyers.

Google Search Console Is Free and Invaluable

If your site is already live, Google Search Console shows you exactly which queries are bringing visitors to your site — and which pages they land on. This is real data from your actual audience, not a tool's estimate. Filter by queries where you're ranking in positions 5–20. Those are your fastest opportunities to move to page one.

Study Your Competitors

Type your competitor's URL into Google's free keyword planner (requires a Google Ads account, but you don't need to run ads). It shows you which keywords Google associates with their site. Take the most relevant ones for your own content.

Answer the Public and Reddit

Answer the Public (free tier available) shows you questions people ask about any topic. Reddit searches show you what your target audience actually discusses, in their own language. Both are free and incredibly useful for content ideas.

The fix: Spend 30 minutes with Google Autocomplete and Search Console before buying any tool. You'll find more actionable keywords than most paid tools provide.
