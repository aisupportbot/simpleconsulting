How to Manage Chargebacks From Major Retailers

By Scott Nguyen · Published May 1, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. Identify the deduction
  2. Match supporting evidence
  3. Assess eligibility and deadlines
  4. Use the pattern to improve operations

Retail deductions should be reconciled with the underlying order and shipment records. A deduction may be valid, already adjusted or potentially disputable; documentation and the retailer’s rules determine the next step.

Identify the deduction

Record the retailer, invoice, purchase order, amount, reason code and notice date. Use the relevant vendor agreement or routing guide to understand the stated basis.

Match supporting evidence

Compare the deduction with the shipment record, bill of lading, receiving information, ASN and prior credits as applicable. Investigate differences before describing the amount as recoverable.

Assess eligibility and deadlines

Check the applicable dispute route and time limit. Submit only where the evidence and rules support a case, and track the actual decision. There is no assumed approval rate or guaranteed reimbursement.

Use the pattern to improve operations

Recurring documented issues may point to a labelling, routing, timing or data problem. Give the corrective action an owner and review whether it reduces the same issue in later shipments.
