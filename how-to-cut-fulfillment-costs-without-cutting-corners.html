How to Cut Fulfillment Costs Without Cutting Corners

By Scott Nguyen · Published Dec 11, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-15 · 1 min read

In this article
  1. Right-Size Your Packaging
  2. Negotiate Your Carrier Rates
  3. Reduce Dunnage and Void Fill
  4. Zone Skipping for High-Volume SKUs
  5. Reduce Your Return Processing Cost

Fulfillment costs are one of the largest variable expenses in e-commerce, and most businesses are paying more than they should. Here's where the savings are — without compromising the customer experience.

Right-Size Your Packaging

Carriers charge by dimensional weight, not just actual weight. A product shipped in a box twice its size pays twice the dimensional weight cost. Audit your packaging by SKU and eliminate oversized boxes. The savings compound across thousands of shipments.

Negotiate Your Carrier Rates

Request carrier or 3PL quotes using your actual parcel volumes, dimensions, destinations and service requirements. Compare base rates, surcharges, pickup and claims terms rather than assuming a standard discount.

Reduce Dunnage and Void Fill

Void fill material is a cost most people don't think about but it adds up. The solution is better-fitted packaging, not more void fill. Packaging that fits the product eliminates the need for excessive fill material.

Zone Skipping for High-Volume SKUs

If you ship a high volume of units to the same region, zone skipping — consolidating shipments at a hub closer to the delivery zone — can reduce per-unit shipping cost significantly. Ask your carrier or 3PL if this is an option for your top SKUs.

Reduce Your Return Processing Cost

Returns create transport, inspection and processing costs. Compare prevention and processing improvements using the actual causes, costs and customer impact in your business.

The next step: Review fulfilment cost per order and as a share of revenue alongside order mix and margin. Investigate the largest drivers rather than applying one cost threshold to every business.
