The 80/20 Rule Applied to E-commerce: Where to Focus for Maximum Results

By Scott Nguyen · Published Aug 28, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. Your Product Pareto
  2. Your Customer Pareto
  3. Your Channel Pareto
  4. Your Marketing Pareto

The 80/20 rule is a prioritisation heuristic, not a fixed distribution to assume in every business. Use your actual contribution and workload data to identify where attention may have the greatest value.

Your Product Pareto

Pull your sales and margin data by SKU. In almost every business, a small number of products drive a disproportionate share of revenue and margin. These are your hero SKUs. They deserve disproportionate investment in listing optimization, advertising, and inventory depth. The long tail of products that generate minimal contribution often creates more complexity than revenue.

Your Customer Pareto

Compare customer contribution, repeat purchasing and servicing cost. Use supported evidence and appropriate permissions when deciding which retention efforts to prioritise.

Your Channel Pareto

If you're selling across multiple channels — Amazon, Shopify, Walmart, wholesale — pull your profit by channel, not just revenue. The channel that generates the most revenue often isn't the most profitable once you account for fees, returns, and margin. Invest in the most profitable channel first.

Your Marketing Pareto

Compare marketing activity using relevant sales or qualified enquiries, cost and measurement limits. Concentrate effort where the evidence supports it without ignoring the role of assisted journeys or new tests.

The next step: Review profit and workload by product, customer and channel. Identify a small set of priorities from the actual distribution, then test the effect of changing effort or investment.
