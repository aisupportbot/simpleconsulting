What I review

Reconcile first. Assess eligibility next.

A difference between records can have several explanations, including timing, an adjustment already made, or a documentation gap. The review checks the evidence before treating a discrepancy as a potential claim.

  • Inbound shipments — quantities sent, received and reconciled
  • Inventory adjustments — lost or damaged units and any credits already issued
  • Returns — refunds, return status, inventory movements and applicable policy
  • Fees — charged amounts compared with the relevant records and fee basis
  • Removals and disposals — instructions, tracking and recorded outcomes
  • WFS settlements — Walmart-specific reports, adjustments and documentation

Reimbursements are not guaranteed

Not every discrepancy qualifies, and not every submitted claim is approved. Eligibility depends on the records, applicable marketplace policies and deadlines. The marketplace decides whether a claim is approved.

Common questions

Before the review

Can you guarantee a reimbursement?

No. A discrepancy does not automatically qualify for reimbursement. Documentation, applicable marketplace policies, claim deadlines and the marketplace’s decision determine the outcome.

What records do you review?

The records depend on the issue and channel. They may include inventory, shipment reconciliation, returns, prior reimbursements and settlement reports.

How far back can a claim be filed?

Deadlines vary by marketplace and claim type. The applicable rules and dates are checked before a claim is recommended.

Do you need my password?

No. Start with relevant exported reports. If later work needs account access, use the marketplace’s own permission controls.

What does the first review cost?

The initial review is free. Any further work is scoped and priced with you before it begins.
