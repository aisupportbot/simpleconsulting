How to Manage Cross-Border E-commerce Into Canada

By Scott Nguyen · Published Jul 24, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. Do not apply one de minimis rule to every shipment
  2. Confirm tax and importer responsibilities
  3. Prepare accurate records
  4. Compare local and cross-border fulfilment

A Canadian cross-border plan should identify the shipping route, importer responsibilities, product classification and customer delivery experience before goods move.

Do not apply one de minimis rule to every shipment

Canadian relief thresholds depend on the route and shipment conditions. CBSA distinguishes mail from courier and provides different treatment for certain courier shipments from the US and Mexico. Duty relief and tax relief also have different thresholds. Check the CBSA guidance for the exact shipment rather than assuming every order below one amount is exempt.

Confirm tax and importer responsibilities

GST/HST and importer obligations depend on the selling and fulfilment arrangement. Do not assume a single annual-sales number resolves a non-resident seller’s obligations. Ask the appropriate tax adviser and customs broker to review the proposed structure.

Prepare accurate records

Confirm product descriptions, tariff classification, country of origin, declared value and the required commercial documents. Agree on who pays duties, taxes and handling charges and how those costs are communicated to buyers.

Compare local and cross-border fulfilment

Model freight, processing, returns, inventory and working capital for each option. Use current rules and quotes; past US import thresholds or rates should not be carried into a new model without verification.
