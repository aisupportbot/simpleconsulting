How to Write Website Copy That Converts Visitors Into Clients

By Scott Nguyen · Published Sep 11, 2024 · Updated 2026-09-15

In this article
  1. Lead With the Customer's Problem, Not Your Solution
  2. Replace Jargon With Specificity
  3. Write for Scanners, Not Readers
  4. Your Call to Action Should Say What Happens Next

The most common problem with small business website copy is that it's written from the inside out — it describes what the business does rather than what the customer gets. Here's how to rewrite it from the outside in.

Lead With the Customer's Problem, Not Your Solution

Instead of "We provide comprehensive e-commerce consulting services," try "Tired of watching your Amazon sales plateau while competitors grow?" The first sentence is about you. The second is about them. Every visitor to your site has a problem. Lead with it.

Replace Jargon With Specificity

Specificity should describe real work and supported evidence. For example, “We review your product listings, inventory and campaign reports, then agree on the priorities” explains the service without inventing a result.

Write for Scanners, Not Readers

Most website visitors scan before they read. Use short paragraphs, bold key phrases, bullet points for lists, and clear headings that communicate the message even if the body copy is skipped. If someone reads only your headings and bolds, they should still understand your value proposition.

Your Call to Action Should Say What Happens Next

"Learn More" is a weak call to action. "Book Your Free 30-Minute Consultation" tells the visitor exactly what will happen when they click. Specific CTAs convert better than vague ones.

The next step: Write a headline that makes the service, audience and useful outcome clear. Ask a prospective buyer what they understand from it, then use enquiry data to assess the change.
