How to Build a KPI Dashboard That Actually Drives Decisions

By Scott Nguyen · Published Feb 19, 2025

In this article
  1. Start With Decisions, Not Data
  2. Fewer Metrics, Better Focus
  3. Make It Visual and Scannable
  4. Build in Automatic Alerts

A KPI dashboard that nobody acts on is just a decoration. Here's how to build one that actually changes what your team does every week.

Start With Decisions, Not Data

The fatal mistake in dashboard design is starting with the data you have. Start instead with the decisions you need to make each week. What would you need to know to decide whether to increase ad spend? To reorder inventory? To flag a fulfillment problem? Build the dashboard around those questions.

Fewer Metrics, Better Focus

A dashboard with 40 metrics tells you nothing. A dashboard with 7 metrics tells you everything. For most e-commerce businesses, the core metrics are: revenue vs target, gross margin, conversion rate, ROAS, inventory days on hand, return rate, and customer acquisition cost. Start there.

Make It Visual and Scannable

You should be able to look at your dashboard for 30 seconds and know whether your business is healthy or not. Use colour coding — green for on target, yellow for approaching threshold, red for problem. Numbers in isolation are harder to process than numbers in context.

Build in Automatic Alerts

The best dashboards don't require you to check them — they notify you when something needs attention. Set threshold alerts for your most critical metrics. An email when ROAS drops below your target is more valuable than a daily dashboard check.

The fix: List the five business decisions you make most often. Build your dashboard to answer those five questions directly. Remove everything else.
